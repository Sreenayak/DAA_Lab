Quick Sort

Time Complexity:
Best Case: O(n log n)
Average Case: O(n log n)
Worst Case: O(n²)


Space Complexity:
O(log n) (Average Case – Recursive Call Stack)
O(n) (Worst Case – When recursion becomes highly unbalanced)


Conclusion:
        Quick Sort is a fast, efficient, and in-place comparison-based sorting algorithm that also follows
the Divide and Conquer approach. It works by selecting a pivot element and partitioning the array into sma
ller subarrays based on the pivot value. It is one of the most commonly used sorting algorithms because of 
its excellent average-case performance and low memory requirements. However, its performance depends on the 
choice of the pivot element, and poor pivot selection can lead to unbalanced partitions and reduced efficie
ncy. Despite this limitation, Quick Sort remains one of the fastest sorting algorithms for practical applic
ations and is widely used in software libraries and operating systems.
